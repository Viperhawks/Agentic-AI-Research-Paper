Beyond Conceptual Retrofitting: The Foundations of Agentic AI

A foundational deconstruction and rigorous methodological synthesis of agentic artificial intelligence — philosophical foundations of agency, multi-agent orchestration topologies, the standardized protocol stack, and trajectory-aware evaluation methodology.

Abstract

The contemporary AI landscape is migrating from passive, prompt-driven language models toward autonomous agentic systems and multi-agent collectives — a transition plagued by conceptual retrofitting.

Abstract

This paper presents a foundational deconstruction and rigorous methodological synthesis of agentic artificial intelligence. Philosophically, we re-examine agency through Dennett's intentional stance and Bandura's social cognitive theory, demonstrating that modern LLM agents possess operational, emergent agency rather than formal symbolic intentionality. Epistemologically, we analyze environmental perception, contrasting semantically repairable linguistic perturbations with the catastrophic collapse of factual grounding induced by external tool-execution failure. Architecturally, we formulate a taxonomy of multi-agent orchestration across centralized, decentralized, hierarchical, and dynamic topologies, dissecting the trade-off between first-class checkpointed state graphs and emergent conversational transcript histories. We formalize the emerging protocol stack, demonstrating why the vertical actuation of MCP and the horizontal peer negotiation of A2A represent mutually indispensable, structurally distinct coordination tiers. Finally, we challenge conventional outcome-based benchmarks with a trajectory-aware evaluation paradigm centred on Stability-Gated Accuracy (SGA) and entropy-injection dynamics, charting a roadmap toward neuro-symbolic hybrid intelligence.

The thesis

The field's vocabulary was borrowed from classical symbolic AI and mapped onto stochastic next-token substrates. That mapping is a category error — and it hides the real engineering problems.

01 / RETROFITTING

Concepts are not architectures

An LLM prompt is not an agent's belief. A JSON-RPC tool invocation is not an intention. Labeling generative pipelines with classical symbolic terms describes behaviour rhetorically, not structurally.

02 / AGENCY

Operational, not formal, agency

Modern LLM agents possess operational, emergent agency rather than formal symbolic intentionality. They fail stochastically — hallucination, semantic drift, reasoning collapse — not predictably.

03 / RESET

An epistemological reset

Separating genuine architectural novelty from cognitive illusion is a precondition for enterprise deployment, mission-critical operation, and trustworthy autonomy.

Philosophical foundations

Agency is a relational attribution grounded in behavioural capability and environmental interaction — not an intrinsic property that emerges from model scale.

Dennett, 1971 — Intentional Stance

Three postures for predicting behaviour

  • Physical stance — semiconductor physics, floating-point arithmetic
  • Design stance — source code, control-flow algorithms
  • Intentional stance — beliefs, desires, rational action

Classically, design and intentional stances were formally aligned. With LLMs they diverge: practitioners take the intentional stance because output mirrors deliberation, yet at design level there are no discrete mental representations.

Bandura, 1986 — Social Cognitive Theory

Four core properties of agency

  • Intentionality — action plans and strategies
  • Forethought — temporal projection of goals
  • Self-reactiveness — execution monitoring, self-regulation
  • Self-reflectiveness — evaluating thought and action

In LLM agents these capabilities are externalised simulations — forced by a software scaffold that re-encodes prior output as newly conditioned input context.

Epistemic grounding

How does an agent keep an accurate, verifiable representation of external reality through API calls, database queries, and document parsers?

Nuser · USER-SIDE PERTURBATIONS

Semantically repairable

Ambiguous prompts, contradictory multi-turn instructions, conversational topic drift, lexical redundancy, boundary probing. These operate at the linguistic level, where dense pre-trained world knowledge and semantic priors fill the inferential gap.

Ntool · TOOL-SIDE PERTURBATIONS

Catastrophic factual collapse

Execution exceptions, malformed or truncated JSON, missing attributes, erroneous returns, misleading signals. These sever the agent's objective perceptual channel to external reality — internal weights cannot infer an unobserved environmental state.

Principle of Asymmetric Epistemic Resilience

Language model agents are remarkably robust against user-side noise but catastrophically fragile against tool-side disruptions. Rather than executing formal exception recovery, autoregressive models frequently rationalise anomalous tool output, fabricating spurious logical chains to bridge the epistemic vacuum.

Formal framework & notation

The paper's claims are stated over a compact vocabulary: agents, their observation channels, state representations, and protocol invariants.

𝔄 = ⟨S, O, π⟩
An agent is a triple of a state space S, an observation space O (linguistic context and tool-returned signals), and a policy π implemented as autoregressive next-token distribution over O.
τ = ⟨a₁ … aₜ⟩
A trajectory: the ordered sequence of actions taken by an agent over an episode, including intermediate tool invocations and their returns.
Nᵤ, Nₜ
Two noise classes: user-side perturbations (ambiguity, contradiction, drift) acting on the linguistic channel, and tool-side perturbations (exceptions, malformed payloads, erroneous data) acting on the actuation channel.
ρ(Nᵤ) ≫ ρ(Nₜ)
The asymmetry claim: resilience of factual grounding to user-side noise is substantially greater than to tool-side noise, because the latter severs the objective perceptual channel to external reality.
G = (V, E, σ)
A checkpointed state graph over nodes V and typed edges E, with durable checkpoints σ enabling deterministic rollback, replay, and human-in-the-loop interruption.
𝒫 = ⟨MCP, A2A⟩
The protocol stack: a vertical tier (agent↔tool actuation, JSON-RPC 2.0) and a horizontal tier (peer negotiation, Agent Cards, stateful task lifecycles).

Stability-Gated Accuracy

Given a trajectory τ with step-wise validity indicators vᵢ ∈ {0,1} and a task-level outcome score s ∈ [0,1]:

SGA(τ) = s · ∏i=1..t vᵢ   —   τ = ⟨a₁ … aₜ⟩

Any intermediate validity failure zeroes the episode score. Entropy injection perturbs the observation channel at step k and measures the divergence of the downstream trajectory, yielding a stability curve rather than a single point estimate.

Orchestration topologies

The structural pattern of an autonomous collective determines its scalability ceiling, token cost profile, failure recovery semantics, and fault tolerance.

STATE MECHANICS — CHECKPOINTED GRAPHS

State as a first-class typed object

Exemplified by LangGraph: state travels along directed edges with durable checkpoints (SQLite / Redis), enabling deterministic rollback, replay, and human-in-the-loop interruption.

STATE MECHANICS — EMERGENT TRANSCRIPTS

State as a conversational log

Exemplified by AutoGen: fine-grained rollback becomes impossible, and context saturation incurs severe cost and coherence penalties as histories grow.

TOPOLOGY 01

Centralized

Single orchestrator holds global state and dispatches work; strong consistency, single point of failure.

TOPOLOGY 02

Decentralized

Peer agents negotiate directly; resilient and horizontally scalable, harder to audit globally.

TOPOLOGY 03

Hierarchical

Tiered supervisors delegate and verify sub-tasks; balances control with specialisation depth.

TOPOLOGY 04

Dynamic

Roles and topology reconfigure at runtime to match task structure and remaining context budget.

The standardized protocol stack

Two structurally distinct coordination tiers — vertically actuated and horizontally negotiated — that are mutually indispensable.

VERTICAL TIER

MCP — Model Context Protocol

Governs vertical agent-to-tool actuation over JSON-RPC 2.0, exposing external world state through a standardised interface.

  • Agent ↔ tool / resource boundary
  • Uniform actuation semantics
  • Where grounding is won or lost
HORIZONTAL TIER

A2A — Agent-to-Agent Protocol

Governs horizontal peer-to-peer collaboration across organisational boundaries using Agent Cards and stateful task lifecycles.

  • Agent ↔ agent negotiation
  • Capability discovery via Agent Cards
  • Stateful task lifecycle and handoff

Trajectory-aware evaluation

Conventional static outcome metrics reward stochastic luck. To enforce procedural integrity, evaluation must inspect the trajectory, not only the destination.

Stability-Gated Accuracy (SGA)

Execution scores collapse to zero if any intermediate step-wise validity indicator fails. Coupled with entropy-injection dynamics, SGA measures whether an agent's process remains stable under induced uncertainty — the operational signature of a system that can be trusted to repeatably reach a correct answer.

01

Beyond outcome metrics

Final-answer accuracy alone cannot distinguish reasoning from lucky retrieval.

02

Step-wise validity gates

Every intermediate indicator must hold for the trajectory to score at all.

03

Entropy injection

Inject uncertainty to observe stability dynamics under realistic noise.

Strategic roadmap

A path toward neuro-symbolic hybrid intelligence, where symbolic verifiability and generative adaptability converge.

1

Adopt trajectory-aware evaluation as default

Replace outcome-only benchmarks with SGA so procedural integrity is measured, not assumed.

2

Instrument the tool boundary

Treat tool-side failures as first-class epistemic events — detect collapse early rather than rationalising anomalies.

3

Choose a state model deliberately

Checkpointed graphs where rollback, replay, and human interruption matter; transcripts where fluid dialogue suffices.

4

Standardise on MCP + A2A

Vertical actuation and horizontal negotiation as separate but cooperating coordination tiers.

5

Converge toward neuro-symbolic hybridity

Combine symbolic verifiability with generative adaptability to build trustworthy, auditable autonomous systems.

Scope, limitations & threats to validity

Stated explicitly, as befits a synthesis of this kind — what the argument does and does not establish.

SCOPE

Methodological synthesis

The paper formalises and systematises existing findings rather than reporting new experiments. Claims are derivable from the cited literature, not from independent measurement.

LIMITATION

Framework-dependent asymmetry

The resilience asymmetry is demonstrated for tool-augmented LLM agents under AgentNoiseBench-style conditions. Results may not generalise to non-LLM agents, or to harnesses with explicit exception-handling logic.

THREAT

Protocol immaturity

MCP and A2A are evolving specifications. Tier boundaries and invariants described here may shift as the standards mature, and the taxonomy is not yet validated against deployed systems.

THREAT

Construct validity of SGA

Step-wise validity indicators must be specified per task domain. A poorly calibrated gate can mask as much as it detects, so gating criteria require independent validation.

FUTURE WORK

Neuro-symbolic convergence

Symbolic verifiability supplying monitoring and proof obligations, while generative models supply adaptability — the trajectory the paper charts rather than one it completes.

OPEN Q

Unresolved questions

Where exactly does the boundary between “orchestrated behavioural illusion” and genuine emergent agency lie, and what empirical test would settle it?
